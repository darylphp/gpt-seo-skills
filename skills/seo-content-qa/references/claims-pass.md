# Claims and Commercial Pass (Pass 2)

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. List every material claim in the exact revision

A claim is material when a reader could rely on it to decide, buy, book, trust or act. Look beyond the body copy: headings, the opening, tables, callouts, captions, image text, alt text, testimonials, tool output, the call to action and its button text, link text, the title, the meta description, author, reviewer and date lines, and any supplied structured data. Give each claim a check ID such as `QC-01`, with its location and its exact wording in this revision.

**Google guidance [C02]:** Google says it is critical to manually fact-check and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing, and that the review also covers title elements, meta descriptions, structured data and image alt text.

## 2. Match each claim to its record

Find the claim's ID in the approved dossier, with its permitted meaning, conditions, attribution and status. A claim marker in the draft, such as `[CL-05]`, points to a record; check the sentence against that record like any other claim. A marker on a sentence that has drifted from its record is a finding, and a sentence with no marker is still checked. Give one result:

| Result | When | Default severity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Matches | The wording stays within the permitted meaning, and every condition is present at this location | None |
| Missing condition | The claim is usable only with a condition, and this location drops it | Publication blocker |
| Overstated | The wording goes beyond the permitted meaning: broader, more certain, more general, comparative or causal | Publication blocker |
| Not in dossier | No claim record covers it | Publication blocker if material. Return to the evidence owner |
| Do not use | The dossier lists the claim as not usable | Publication blocker |
| Stale | Its evidence has passed a review trigger, or a commercial fact has not been rechecked close to release | Publication blocker until rechecked |
| Not verified | The evidence needed to check it was not supplied or could not be opened | Publication blocker for a core claim. Otherwise remove or defer the claim |

A claim that is not in the dossier can sometimes be checked from supplied evidence by the same rules the dossier uses: an owner's record for a business fact, the primary source and exact passage for a public fact, original records for a result. Record the source, the location and the date, and still return the claim to the evidence owner so the dossier covers it. Never clear a claim because it is plausible, widely repeated, on a competitor page, generated by AI, approved for branding or pressed by the user. An owner's approval sets the business position; it does not verify a technical, comparative or scientific claim.

If no approved dossier exists, check against the supplied records with the same tests and say so in section 7. If nothing can be opened for the core claims, the status is **Blocked: Supply the evidence**.

## 3. Figures

Check units, currency, tax basis (for example, including GST), denominator, sample, period, calculation and rounding. Recalculate every percentage from its counts. A range never becomes a single exact figure. A condition a figure depends on, such as home size, plan or eligibility, travels with it at every location, including the meta description and image text.

## 4. Quotes, testimonials and people

- A quote matches its source wording, speaker and context, within the permission given (for example, first name and suburb only).
- A testimonial stays one customer's experience. It never becomes a heading, a general result or a comparison with competitors.
- An author or reviewer line names a real person in their real role who actually wrote or reviewed this revision. **Google guidance [C01]:** Google encourages accurate bylines where readers would expect them and treats fabricated creator profiles, such as invented credentials, as deceptive.
- A first-person, testing or "we found" claim needs a record that the work happened.

## 5. Comparisons and reviews

A comparison needs comparable versions or conditions and stated criteria, with drawbacks as well as strengths. A comparison with "other inspectors", "competitors" or "the rest" needs comparable evidence about them, which one customer's experience or one competitor's page does not provide.

**Google guidance [C04]:** For reviews, Google recommends showing your own experience with evidence such as photos, giving measurements, covering benefits and drawbacks from your own research, and explaining with first-hand evidence why something is "best". A source-led page must describe its research basis and never imply hands-on testing.

## 6. Commercial facts

Check prices, fees, tax basis, availability, service area, terms, guarantees, offers and effective dates against the current record, and recheck them close to release. Look for conflicting values between the body, tables, release fields, image text, downloads and structured data. A call to action must not promise more than the offer: "free", "instant" or "same day" are claims. Note the destination of each call to action and link as supplied, and leave the working check to publishing.

## 7. Regulated and high-consequence claims

For health, medical, safety, legal, financial, environmental, accessibility or other regulated claims, record the market, the qualified review the claim needs, and whether a review record for this exact revision was supplied. This skill gives no legal, medical, financial or safety clearance and does not decide whether a claim meets any law, code or standard. A core claim without its required review is a publication blocker returned to the specialist reviewer. An optional one can be removed instead.

## 8. Structured data facts

**Google guidance [C11]:** Google's general structured data guidelines say markup must represent the page's visible content, must not describe content readers cannot see, and must not be irrelevant or misleading, such as fabricated reviews. Correct markup does not guarantee a rich result.

When structured data is supplied, check each fact in it against the visible, cleared claim it repeats. A mismatch is a finding. Do not write production markup; hand the decision to schema review with the claim IDs it may use.
