# Editorial Pass (Pass 1)

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** or **W3C guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. What the pass checks

Read the exact revision against the approved brief, section by section.

| Check | What to look for |
| --- | --- |
| Reader fit and opening | Does the opening say what the page offers, who it is for and what to do next, in the brief's terms? Scene-setting that could open any page ("In today's busy world") is filler |
| Section purpose and order | Every section the brief plans is present, does its stated job, and sits where the reader needs it. Run each section's completion test from the brief and record the result |
| Meaning | Each sentence says one thing clearly. No contradictions between sections, no ambiguous figures or pronouns, no sentence whose meaning changed from the brief's key point |
| Natural, specific language | Filler, repeated summaries, stacked adjectives, forced lists of three, empty transitions, and lines that could describe any business |
| Voice | The brief's market spelling (Australian English when none is set), point of view, authorised first person, controlled terms and prohibited words |
| Headings | Each heading describes its section. The hierarchy makes sense. No heading claims more than its section supports |
| Call to action and links | The call to action wording and destination match the brief. Link text says where the link goes |
| Scope and preservation | Nothing outside the page boundary. For an Improve action, useful existing material is kept. Qualifications, disclosures, attributions and conditions are intact |
| Release fields | The title, meta description, captions and alt text read naturally, describe this page or image, and are not keyword lists |

## 2. Claim passages go to pass 2

Pass 1 does not decide whether a claim is true. Mark a passage for pass 2 when:

- a claim's wording differs from its approved meaning in the brief or dossier, or drops a condition;
- a claim appears that is not in the brief's evidence controls;
- an edit, by anyone, touched a claim, comparison, price, promise, attribution or disclosure.

Never improve an unsupported statement into more confident or more polished wording. When a completion test fails because a claim's condition is missing, a proposed edit may restore the dossier's approved meaning and conditions. The passage stays marked for pass 2.

## 3. Proposed edits

- Work at passage level: quote the current text, give the proposed text, and give the reason in a few words.
- Change wording, not facts. Add no claim, figure, name, place, credential, example or quote that is not in the brief and dossier.
- Keep each claim's ID and conditions with its passage.
- Never rewrite the whole page. When a section is missing, the order needs rebuilding, or the page needs content the evidence does not provide, return it to the writer or the brief owner instead.
- Proposed edits are proposals. A person applies or rejects them and approves the resulting revision before pass 2.

If every finding is resolved by proposed edits, the pass state is `Cleared with proposed edits`. If any finding needs the writer, the brief owner or new evidence, the state is `Corrections needed`.

## 4. Titles and meta descriptions

**Google guidance [C05]:** Google recommends a descriptive, concise title element specific to each page, without repeated keywords or boilerplate shared across many pages. It sets no character limit for title elements, truncates title links to fit the device, and may build the title link from other sources, including the main visible heading.

**Google guidance [C06]:** Google mainly builds snippets from the page's own text and may use the meta description when it gives a more accurate summary. Each page should have its own accurate description, and a string of keywords does not help. There is no maximum length, and long snippets are truncated to fit.

Character or pixel counts can guide layout. Do not fail a title or description on length alone, and do not promise that the supplied text will be shown. Any fact in a title or description is a claim, so mark it for pass 2.

## 5. Images, captions and alt text

**Google guidance [C07]:** Google recommends useful, descriptive alt text that fits the page's context, warns against keyword stuffing in alt text, and recommends placing images near text that describes them.

**W3C guidance [C08]:** The W3C alt decision tree gives an informative image a short description of its meaning, a functional image in a link or button the destination or action, a purely decorative image an empty `alt`, an image of text the text itself unless it appears nearby, and a complex image its information elsewhere on the page.

Describe what the image shows from its asset record. Never add a place, person, date or result the record does not support. A caption or alt text that states a fact is a claim, so mark it for pass 2.

## 6. What pass 1 does not use

AI-detector scores, an "AI percentage", readability-score targets, keyword density, entity counts and word counts are not editorial tests. **Google guidance [C01]:** Google says it has no preferred word count. Natural editing improves reading; it is not proof of human authorship or of accuracy. Do not loop on requests to make the page "sound more human". Improve what a reader would notice, then stop.
