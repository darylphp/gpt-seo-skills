# Value and Policy Pass (Pass 3)

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. Does the page complete its task?

Take the brief's reader outcome and each section's completion test. Record each test as Pass, Issue or Not verified, with the passage that settles it. Then ask whether the page would still be useful to a visitor who arrives directly, not from a search.

**Google guidance [C01]:** Google's self-assessment questions ask whether content gives original information, research or analysis, whether it is free of easily verified factual errors, and whether a visitor would leave having learned enough to reach their goal. It treats content made mainly to attract search visits, or that mostly summarises what others say without adding much value, as warning signs.

## 2. Is the promised contribution on the page?

Name the passage, table, photo or tool that delivers the brief's differentiator. A promise in the introduction is not delivery. A longer page, more headings, a citation list, or keyword and entity coverage is not a contribution by itself.

**Google guidance [C13]:** Google's guide to its generative AI features says content with a distinct viewpoint, such as first-hand experience or expert insight, is likely to shape a site's presence there more than any other tip, and that generic material repeating what is already available adds little.

## 3. Is the page distinct from its neighbours?

Compare the revision with the nearest existing or planned pages and any template siblings named in the brief or page decision, from supplied text or pages that can be opened. Check that the page does not serve the same task as a neighbour, keeps to its boundary, and links rather than repeats where the brief says so. Shared template text adds the same thing everywhere it appears; the page's own sections have to do the work.

If a neighbour named as the boundary cannot be seen, the distinctness check is `Not verified`. When distinctness is a core risk, such as a location or template page, the status is **Blocked: Supply the evidence** until the neighbour's text is supplied. Never use a uniqueness percentage. For a page set, check this page, record which parts are shared, and send decisions about the set to site architecture work.

## 4. Spam policies, against what is on the page

**Google guidance [C03]:** Google's spam policies describe, among others:

- **Doorway abuse:** pages made to rank for similar queries that send people to less useful intermediate pages, such as several city or region pages that funnel to one page.
- **Scaled content abuse:** many pages made mainly to manipulate rankings with little value, however they are produced, including generated, stitched-together or lightly transformed content.
- **Site reputation abuse:** third-party content published on a host site mainly to benefit from the host's ranking signals. Third-party content alone is not a violation.
- **Thin affiliation:** affiliate pages that copy merchant descriptions without original value.
- **Hidden text and links:** content placed for search engines rather than people, such as text matching the background or positioned off screen. Accordions, tabs and screen-reader text are not violations.
- **Keyword stuffing:** keywords or numbers repeated unnaturally or out of context, including blocks listing places a page is trying to rank for.
- **Misleading functionality:** a tool or feature that does not do what it says.
- **Scraping:** content taken from other sites and republished without added value.

The policy page does not treat AI-generated content as a violation by itself. **Google guidance [C02]:** Google says using generative AI to create many pages without adding value for users may violate its scaled content abuse policy.

Operating method for one page:

- Judge the page's actual purpose and value on the evidence in front of you. Name the policy and the passage or practice behind any concern.
- AI assistance, third-party authorship or repeated template text is not a violation by itself. One page cannot show a site-wide problem.
- An accurate service-area list that readers use to check coverage, shown once, is different from a block of place names written to rank. Check it against the approved coverage claim.
- An invented office, address or local presence is a misleading claim. Return it through pass 2, and send Business Profile questions to local SEO work.
- Write `No concern found in the inspected scope` rather than "compliant". This is not a manual action forecast, a legal view or a guarantee of policy immunity.

## 5. Presentation and usability

Operating method adapted from the Visual Semantics and Page Presentation playbook:

- The main content and the next step are easy to find, and secondary material does not crowd them out.
- Headings group each explanation with its evidence and the action it leads to.
- Prices, conditions, limits and other information a reader decides on are in text, not only in an image or a download.
- Real photos are labelled as what they are. Stock, illustrative or generated images are never presented as proof of real work, people, premises or results.
- Tables compare like with like, with units and dates.
- Alt text follows each image's function [C07, C08].

**Google guidance [C18]:** Google's page experience self-assessment asks whether content displays well on mobile devices, whether ads distract from or interfere with the main content, whether intrusive interstitials are avoided, and whether the layout lets visitors easily tell the main content apart from other content. It says page experience aspects beyond Core Web Vitals do not directly help a site rank higher, while improving the overall experience is still worthwhile.

Mobile layout, keyboard use, hidden elements, interactive sections and loading behaviour can only be checked on a rendered page. If a rendered page or preview was supplied or opened, record the device or viewport and what was checked. Otherwise record `Not checked: rendered page not available` and hand those checks to publishing. This is not an accessibility audit.

## 6. What does not decide this pass

Uniqueness percentages, keyword density, entity coverage, word counts, optimisation or readability scores, AI-detector results, publishing volume and citation counts.

**Google guidance [C13]:** Google says its generative AI features need no special writing style, chunking or AI-specific markup, and that making pages for every query variation mainly to manipulate rankings or AI responses violates its scaled content abuse policy.
