# Claim Records

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. One record per material claim

Give each material claim, or a linked group that one source supports, an ID such as `CL-01`. Record:

- The proposed claim, its exact intended meaning and where it will appear.
- The claim type: factual, causal, numerical, comparative, experiential, commercial, quoted or regulated.
- The source ID, the exact supporting location, the version or date, and the date checked.
- What the source states, kept apart from any inference drawn from it, with the conditions and limits.
- One status from the table below.
- A confidence, an owner, the permitted use and attribution, any required review, and a review trigger.

A claim is material when a reader could rely on it to decide, buy, book, trust or act. Include claims in headings, tables, captions, calls to action, image text, testimonials, titles, meta descriptions and structured data, not only in body copy.

## 2. Statuses

Use these exact labels. They match the claim statuses the SEO Content Brief Skill accepts.

| Status | When | Permitted use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Verified for use | The evidence supports the claim as worded, within its market, time, audience and scope | State the supported fact within that scope |
| Verified with qualification | The evidence supports a narrower or conditional version | Use only with its qualification, attribution or disclosure, in every place it appears |
| Supported inference | A conclusion that follows from labelled, usable premises | Present as analysis, cite the premises and keep the uncertainty visible |
| Approved attributable opinion or experience | A real person's opinion or experience, with their permission | Attribute it to that person, keep its context, and never present it as general fact |
| Client confirmation required | A business fact the responsible owner has not yet confirmed or supplied | Do not use. Raise a proof request |
| Contradicted | Appropriate evidence says otherwise | Do not use. Record the conflicting source and a supported replacement if one exists |
| Unsupported | No appropriate evidence, or only a third-party claim, AI output or a snippet | Do not use |
| Prohibited | Excluded by the business, a supplied rule or a required reviewer | Do not use. Record who excluded it |
| Stale | The evidence was valid but has expired or been superseded | Do not use until rechecked |

These map onto the five statuses in the Content Evidence, Briefing and Publishing QA playbook: supported (Verified for use), supported with qualification (Verified with qualification), awaiting confirmation (Client confirmation required), contradicted (Contradicted) and exclude (Unsupported, Prohibited or Stale). Supported inference and approved attributable opinion or experience keep analysis and testimony apart from fact.

Ambiguous, missing, expired or inaccessible support is not usable. Never promote a status because a claim is plausible, widely repeated, on a competitor page, generated by AI, approved for branding, or pressed by the user. When a claim cannot be used, record a narrower supported replacement as its own claim ID, where one exists.

## 3. Confidence

Record High, Medium or Low with a reason. High confidence means the evidence supports the stated proposition within its scope. It does not mean the outcome is typical, that future results are certain, or that an SEO change caused past performance. A supplied screenshot that has not been checked against its origin is rarely High.

## 4. Figures

Check every number for:

- Units, currency and tax basis (for example, including or excluding GST or VAT).
- The denominator and sample: what was counted, and what was not.
- The period, the date of the data and the date of the claim.
- The calculation and rounding. Recalculate any percentage from its counts.
- Conditions such as size, location, plan, eligibility or minimum spend.

Never turn a range into a single exact figure, and never drop the condition a price or result depends on. A rate observed among customers who chose to buy does not describe everyone.

## 5. Quotes and attributed statements

Check the original wording, the speaker, the context and the permission to quote. Paraphrase when a direct quote is not needed. Never write a quote for a real person. If the page needs a person's words, raise a proof request for them to supply or approve their own wording, and keep it `Client confirmation required` until they do.

## 6. Comparisons and reviews

A comparison needs comparable versions, conditions and stated criteria, with drawbacks and suitability as well as strengths.

**Google guidance [C04]:** Google's reviews guidance recommends evaluating from a user's perspective, providing evidence such as visuals, audio or other links of your own experience, sharing quantitative measurements, discussing benefits and drawbacks based on your own original research, and including first-hand supporting evidence when calling something the best.

**Google guidance [C16]:** Google describes its reviews system as aiming to reward reviews with insightful analysis and original research, written by experts or enthusiasts who know the topic well. It does not evaluate third-party user reviews on a product or service page.

If no first-hand assessment took place, the comparison is source-led and must describe its research basis, never imply hands-on testing.

## 7. Results and case studies

Record the original records, the metric definitions, the comparison period, the conditions and what else changed at the same time. A before-and-after change does not show cause or a typical result. Keep the client's permission and any privacy limits with the record.

## 8. Freshness and review triggers

Give each claim that can change a review trigger: a price list revision, an effective date, a licence or certificate expiry, a staff change, a policy or product update, a new data export, or a set review date. A claim whose trigger has passed becomes `Stale` until rechecked. Recheck commercial facts such as prices, availability and terms close to release.
