# Conflicts and Proof Requests

Everything in this file is operating method, not Google guidance.

## 1. Conflicting evidence

Keep both readings visible: each source, its location, its date and what it says. Resolve the conflict with the appropriate primary source or the responsible owner, not by picking the more convenient figure or the more recent-looking page. Until it is resolved, the dependent claim is `Client confirmation required` (for a business fact) or `Contradicted` (when appropriate evidence already says otherwise).

## 2. Missing or inaccessible evidence

Mark a claim whose source cannot be opened or was not supplied as `Not verified` in the source table, and leave it unusable. A search snippet, another AI's summary or a citation that cannot be traced does not complete the check.

## 3. Choose a route for every unusable claim

| Route | When |
| --- | --- |
| Verify | The right evidence exists and someone can supply it in time |
| Narrow | A smaller claim is supported, such as two named technicians instead of "all our technicians" |
| Qualify | The claim holds only under stated conditions, and those conditions can travel with it |
| Replace | A different, supported statement serves the same reader need |
| Remove | The claim adds nothing the reader needs, or nothing could support it |
| Defer | The claim matters, the evidence is coming, and the affected material can wait |

A disclaimer does not make a false or unsupported statement acceptable. Internal placeholders are fine in a draft, but unresolved placeholders, invented values and unsupported material claims must not reach production.

## 4. Proof requests

Ask for the smallest proof that would settle the claim. Record each request as:

| ID | Claim | Exact fact or asset needed | Why it matters | Acceptable evidence | Who can supply it | Can the page proceed without it? | State |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

"Can the page proceed without it?" is Yes, with what the page loses, or No, with the claim or section that waits. Avoid vague requests such as "add more E-E-A-T", "more trust signals" or "stronger proof". Name the record, the person and the decision it unblocks.

## 5. Decide the dossier status

Sort claims into two groups:

- **Core:** claims without which the page cannot complete the reader's task or deliver its promised contribution, such as what the service includes, who it is for, the price basis or the evidence behind the page's point of difference.
- **Optional:** claims the page can drop or narrow without failing the task, such as an extra statistic, a third testimonial or a superlative.

If every core claim is usable, the dossier is ready, even with optional requests open. Record each dropped optional claim and its effect. If any core claim is unusable, or waits on a qualified review, the status is **Evidence gaps: Proof requests open**.

Choose between the two ready statuses by the contribution, not the number of sources. First-hand work, business records, real expert input or first-party data make an evidence dossier. Public and primary sources alone make a source-led dossier.

## 6. Research notes

An interesting but unproven idea, a figure that might be checked later or a competitor claim worth testing goes in a research note, kept apart from the claim register. A research note is never production copy.
