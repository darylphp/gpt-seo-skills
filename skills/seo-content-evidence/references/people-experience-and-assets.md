# People, Experience and Assets

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. Authorship and responsibility

**Google guidance [C01]:** Google strongly encourages accurate authorship information, such as bylines, where readers might expect it, and warns against fabricated creator profiles. E-E-A-T itself is not a specific ranking factor, and of its aspects, trust is the most important. Google also says its quality rater guidelines are not a guide to ranking first, and that raters' ratings do not directly influence ranking [C02].

Record the real people behind the page and what each did: wrote, contributed, tested, reviewed or approved, and for which revision. A company service page does not need an invented individual byline. A reviewer must have actually reviewed the relevant revision and have expertise that fits the claims attributed to them. Never add a portrait, credential, biography or author entity to create an impression of involvement.

## 2. Experience and testing

A first-person, testing or "we found" claim needs a record of who did the work, what was done, when, how, on what sample, with what result and what limits. Without it, the claim is `Unsupported`, and the page describes its research basis instead.

**Google guidance [C01]:** Google says readers trust reviews more when they understand how the work was done, such as the number of products tested and the test results, accompanied by evidence such as photographs.

**Google guidance [C13]:** Google's guide to its generative AI features says content people find unique, compelling and useful is likely to influence a site's presence there more than any of its other suggestions. It gives a first-hand review as an example of a unique perspective, and advises against recycling what others have already said.

## 3. Expert contributions

Use the expert's actual notes, interview or approved wording, their relevant expertise and the exact part they contributed. Record permitted attribution. Never invent a quote, a credential or a reviewer. If the expert has not yet reviewed the claim, it stays unusable for any statement that depends on their judgement.

## 4. AI assistance

Treat every generated source, claim, figure, quote, entity and example as unverified until checked against its source. Review the actual source and the exact passage, not only whether a link exists.

**Google guidance [C02]:** Google says generative AI can help with research and structure, and that generating many pages without adding value for users may violate its scaled content abuse policy [C03]. It says it is critical to manually fact-check and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing, and that this review also applies to title elements, meta description elements, structured data and image alt text. It suggests adding information on how content was created when content is automatically generated, and Google's people-first guide asks whether the use of automation is evident to visitors through disclosures or in other ways [C01].

Record genuine human review where the process requires it. Never claim it happened automatically, and never treat an AI-detector score as evidence of accuracy or authorship.

## 5. Regulated and high-consequence claims

For health, medical, safety, legal, financial, environmental, accessibility or other regulated claims, record:

- The market and the kind of claim.
- The qualified review the market needs (for example, a registered practitioner, a licensed professional or the business's compliance owner).
- The named reviewer, or the role if the name is pending, the review scope and its status.

General source checking, a client's approval or this dossier does not replace that review. This skill does not decide whether a claim meets any law, advertising code or professional rule. If the review is unavailable, narrow, remove or defer the affected claim. If a core claim waits on it, the dossier status is **Evidence gaps: Proof requests open**.

## 6. Assets and permissions

For each image, video, chart, tool output, document, testimonial and case study, record:

| Field | Check |
| --- | --- |
| Provenance | Who made it, when, and from which job, test or source |
| What it shows | What it actually depicts or demonstrates, without overstatement |
| Permission | The licence, the owner's consent, or the customer's consent and its scope (for example, first name and suburb only) |
| Privacy | Names, faces, addresses, house numbers, number plates, documents or health details visible in it |
| Use limits | Where it may appear and with what caption or attribution |

Stock or generated imagery is not evidence that real work, testing, premises or people exist. One testimonial is one customer's experience, not a general claim about results or competitors. Record consent for testimonials and any incentive the customer received.

## 7. Facts headed for structured data

Facts that will feed structured data, such as prices, opening hours, service areas or ratings, need the same claim checks as body copy.

**Google guidance [C11]:** Google's general structured data guidelines say markup must be a true representation of the page content, must not mark up content readers cannot see, and must not mark up irrelevant or misleading content such as fake reviews. Correct markup does not guarantee that a rich result appears.

Hand the markup decision to schema review with the claim IDs it may use. Do not draft production markup in the dossier.
