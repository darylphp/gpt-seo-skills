# Evidence by Claim Type

IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

## 1. Evidence labels

Give every source one label. The label says how the dossier knows about the source, not whether the claim is true.

| Label | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Observed | Opened or fetched during this dossier, with the supporting passage read. Record the URL or file, the date, the location and what it showed. |
| Supplied | Provided by the user and not independently checked: an export, a screenshot, a document, a draft or a note. Record who supplied it and when. |
| Owner statement | A business fact stated or confirmed by the owner or an authorised person, such as a service, a price, a start date or a permission. Record who and when. |
| Third-party claim | What someone else says: a competitor page, an unsourced article, a press release, a forum post, a vendor page or AI output. It shows the claim was made, not that it is true. |
| Inference | A conclusion drawn from labelled premises. Name the premises and keep the uncertainty. |
| Not verified | The check was needed, and the source was unavailable, inaccessible or missing. Never report it as passed. |

## 2. What each kind of claim needs

| Claim or asset | Evidence to collect | Main limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business or service fact | The approved service scope, coverage or locations, terms, credentials and the responsible owner | A claim already published on the site is not substantiated by being published |
| Product or software capability | The exact product and version, current specifications, the actual offer and evidence that the capability exists | Never infer a feature, integration or certification from a competitor or a roadmap |
| Experience or testing | Who did the work, what was done, the date, the method, the sample, the results and the limits | A summary of other people's sources is not first-hand use or testing |
| Expert contribution | The actual notes or interview, the person's relevant expertise, their exact contribution and permitted attribution | Never invent quotes, credentials or reviewer involvement |
| Result or case study | Original records, definitions, the comparison period, conditions, permission and other things that changed at the same time | A before-and-after change does not show cause or a typical result |
| Comparative claim | Comparable versions and conditions, stated criteria, sources and fair limitations | Never choose a winner from commission, word count or unsupported superiority |
| Public fact, statistic or policy | The appropriate primary source, the exact supporting passage, its date or version, and its scope | A source URL is not enough if the passage does not support the wording |
| Image, video, tool or testimonial | Provenance, an accurate description, permission and what it actually demonstrates | Illustrative media or one testimonial is not general proof of performance |

For original research and technical sources, preserve the method, conditions and units. A patent, benchmark or case study does not show how a search engine currently works.

## 3. What each source can and cannot show

| Source | Can show | Cannot show |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Approved service record or scope sheet | What the business offers, where and on what terms, as of its revision | That a technical or safety statement inside it is correct |
| Owner or staff statement | The business position, permission and facts within their knowledge | Independent proof of a scientific, legal, comparative or market-wide claim |
| Job log, CRM or order export | What the business recorded, under its field definitions, for its period | A rate for people who did not buy, or why the result happened |
| Analytics or Search Console export | What was recorded under its configuration and filters | Customer outcomes, satisfaction or cause |
| Customer review or testimonial | One customer's experience and opinion, with permission | How often the experience happens, or a claim about competitors |
| Government, regulator or standards body page | The rule, figure or guidance it states, at its date and in its jurisdiction | That the business complies with it |
| Peer-reviewed or original research | Its findings, under its method, sample and conditions | A result outside those conditions |
| Manufacturer or vendor specification | What the vendor states about its own product version | Independent performance or a comparison |
| Competitor page | What the competitor says about itself | Whether it is true, or how the business compares |
| Press release, unsourced article or listicle | That someone made the claim | The claim itself, unless it cites a primary source you can open |
| AI output or a search snippet | A lead to check | Any fact. Treat every generated source, figure, quote and example as unverified until checked |

**Google guidance [C01]:** Google's self-assessment questions ask whether content provides original information, reporting, research or analysis, whether it presents information in a way that makes readers want to trust it, such as clear sourcing and evidence of the expertise involved, and whether it has easily verified factual errors.

## 4. Source access

Open public sources where the platform allows, within the scope of the page. Record the URL, the date, what was read and the supporting location. If a source cannot be opened, mark the dependent claim `Not verified` and say what would complete the check. Do not log in to accounts, submit forms or contact people on the user's behalf.
