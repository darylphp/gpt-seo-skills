# Writer Routes

The brief chooses the route. This file sets what each route's draft must give the reader and what it must never do. IDs in brackets refer to the [source register](source-register.md). Text marked **Google guidance** paraphrases that source. Everything else is operating method.

Every route also follows the [drafting method](drafting-method.md) and [claims in copy](claims-in-copy.md).

## W1. Local Service Content Writer

**Reader job:** decide whether a real local service can help with their situation, and what to do next.

Give the reader:

- What the service is and isn't, in the business's approved scope, early on.
- Who it suits and who it doesn't, including the dossier's exclusions.
- What happens: the steps, timing and what the customer needs to provide, with their conditions.
- Price, or how pricing works, only as the dossier permits and with its conditions.
- Local proof the dossier permits: the approved service area, real job photos with permission, attributable customer experience.
- A clear next step to the verified contact or booking path.

Check before writing: the service area matches the approved list; credentials name only the verified people; prices keep their tax, size and quote conditions; only the permitted photos are placed.

Never: imply premises, staff or local presence the dossier doesn't support; name places outside the approved area; produce town variants by swapping place names; add a block of suburb or town names for search; borrow a competitor's process as this business's own.

A service area list that readers use to check coverage is fine once, in a form they can scan. **Google guidance [C03]:** Google gives city- or region-targeted pages that funnel users to one page as a doorway abuse example, and blocks of text listing the cities and regions a page is trying to rank for as a keyword stuffing example.

## W2. Affiliate Review and Comparison Writer

**Reader job:** evaluate an independent review, comparison, set of alternatives or buying shortlist, and choose what fits their situation.

Give the reader:

- The evaluation criteria and how each option was assessed, before the verdicts.
- Like-for-like comparisons: the same versions, plans, conditions and units.
- Benefits and drawbacks for each option, including drawbacks of the options the page recommends.
- Who each option suits and who should look elsewhere.
- The basis of the review stated plainly: hands-on testing (what, when, how many and with what results) or research (which sources).
- The commercial relationship, disclosed where the brief requires it and near the recommendation it affects.

Check before writing: each testing claim has a test record in the dossier; each measurement has its unit and conditions; prices and plans have a check date; each "best" claim has its first-hand reason.

Never: imply hands-on use, testing or photos that didn't happen; choose a winner by commission, length or which brand pays; reuse the merchant's product description as the review; hide or soften drawbacks; say "tested" when the dossier says research.

**Google guidance [C04]:** Google's review guidance asks for evidence of your own experience, quantitative measurements, benefits and drawbacks from your own research, comparisons with alternatives and the situations each suits, and first-hand evidence for why something is best. It says to focus on quality and originality rather than length. Google's reviews system aims to reward insightful analysis and original research [C16]. Affiliate pages that copy the merchant's descriptions and reviews without adding value are the spam policies' example of thin affiliation [C03]. Paid links should carry `rel="sponsored"` or `nofollow` [C10]. That attribute is for search engines. It is not a disclosure to readers.

## W3. SaaS and B2B Content Writer

**Reader job:** evaluate a SaaS or B2B offer, feature, use case, integration, implementation or buying decision.

Give the reader:

- What the product does for this use case in approved capability terms, and what it doesn't do.
- Who it fits: team size, existing tools, plan or prerequisites, as recorded.
- How implementation works: steps, effort, dependencies and who does what, as the dossier permits.
- Plans, limits and terms that change the decision, with their check date.
- Proof that fits each claim: a case study with its customer, period and conditions, documentation or a demo record.
- The next step for the buyer's stage, such as documentation, a trial, a demo or sales contact, as approved.

Check before writing: each feature, integration and plan limit has a current record for this version; security, compliance and certification claims each have their own record and scope; customer results keep their customer, period and conditions.

Never: infer a feature or integration from a competitor, a roadmap or a similar product; state a certification, security standard or uptime figure without its record; turn one customer's result into a typical outcome; describe a competitor's product beyond what the dossier supports.

## W4. Ecommerce Category and Collection Writer

**Reader job:** browse a class of products the store actually sells, and narrow the choice.

Give the reader:

- What the collection includes and where it stops, from the current assortment.
- The attributes that really separate the products, such as size, material, compatibility or use, and how to choose between them.
- Short guidance where a choice is commonly hard, as the dossier supports.
- Routes to the subcategories and products the brief approves.

Check before writing: the products and attributes named are in the current assortment; any "most popular" or "best seller" claim has its data, period and definition.

Never: write filler so that each facet, filter or query variation gets its own copy; repeat the collection name and modifiers for search; describe products the store doesn't carry; promise stock, delivery or prices that change faster than the page.

Category copy helps the reader choose. It is not a block of text placed above or below the product grid for search. **Google guidance:** Google recommends linking from categories to subcategories and products so products can be found by following links [C18]. It warns that faceted navigation can create a very large number of URLs, and describes ways to keep unneeded facet URLs from being crawled [C19]. Which filtered views deserve their own indexable page is a site architecture decision, not a writing one.

## W5. Ecommerce Product Detail Page Writer

**Reader job:** evaluate one specific product, or a meaningful group of variants, and decide whether it fits their need.

Give the reader:

- What the product is, who it suits and its main use, early on.
- Specifications exactly as recorded, with units, and which variants differ.
- Compatibility, sizing, care, warranty and contents, as the dossier permits.
- Limitations and who it doesn't suit.
- Offer terms that change the decision, such as price, delivery and returns, only as current records allow.

Check before writing: the brand, model, version and variant match the record; every specification and unit match; compatibility claims name exactly what was confirmed.

Never: round, convert or tidy a specification without the dossier's approval; reuse the manufacturer's description as the page's own text; claim a feature for every variant when only some have it; invent customer reviews, ratings or usage; add health, safety or performance claims beyond the record.

**Google guidance [C02]:** Google notes that Merchant Center has its own policies for AI-generated content, including specifying and labelling AI-generated product titles and descriptions separately. Route product data that feeds Merchant Center through the publishing handoff.

## W6. Expert-Led Article and Thought-Leadership Writer

**Reader job:** understand defensible research, a real expert's contribution, a case, or original analysis of sources.

Give the reader:

- The answer or argument early, then the reasoning and evidence.
- Whose expertise or experience the article rests on, and exactly what each person contributed.
- The method and limits of any research, case or data: who, what, when, sample and conditions.
- Sources named close to the claims that rely on them.
- Where experts or sources disagree, both readings and what would settle the question.

Check before writing: each quote is the expert's own approved wording; each finding has its record and limits; each source passage says what the sentence says; the dossier says whether the page is first-hand or source-led.

Never: invent an interview, quote, credential, reviewer or study; turn a correlation or a before-and-after change into a cause; write a source-led page as if the author used, visited or tested something; present a contested view as settled; give health, legal, financial or safety advice beyond the cleared review.

**Google guidance [C01]:** Google's people-first questions ask whether content provides original information, research or analysis, goes beyond the obvious, adds value instead of copying or rewriting its sources, shows clear sourcing and evidence of expertise, and is free of easily verified factual errors. Its guide to generative AI features gives a first-hand review as an example of a unique perspective and advises against simply summarising what already exists [C13]. Google says trust is the most important part of E-E-A-T, and that its systems give more weight to content with strong E-E-A-T on topics that could significantly affect people's health, finances or safety [C01].

## Route boundaries

The brief's route stands unless the page job changes. A SaaS vendor's own comparison page stays W3. An independent affiliate review of SaaS products is W2. A local firm's research article is W6. A category buying guide that helps browse that collection is W4. A product page whose educational content supports the purchase stays W5. If the draft can't do the reader job inside its route, stop and return to B3.
