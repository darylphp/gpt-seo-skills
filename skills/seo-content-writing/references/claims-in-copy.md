# Claims in Copy

Everything in this file is operating method, not Google guidance. The statuses are the ones the SEO Content Evidence Skill assigns and the SEO Content Brief Skill accepts. Equivalent labels work when the dossier establishes the same permission and evidence quality.

## 1. Usable statuses and how they go into copy

| Dossier status | How it goes into the draft |
| --- | --- |
| Verified for use | State the fact within its market, period, audience and scope. |
| Verified with qualification | Write the qualification into the same sentence, or the one beside it, every time the claim appears. |
| Supported inference | Write it as analysis ("on these figures", "this suggests"), with its premises named and its uncertainty kept. Never as fact. |
| Approved attributable opinion or experience | Attribute it to the real person or customer, using only the name and detail their permission allows, and keep it as their view or experience. |

Claims marked Client confirmation required, Contradicted, Unsupported, Prohibited or Stale stay out of the draft, including headings, captions, CTAs, image text, tables and summaries. Where the dossier names a replacement claim, use the replacement.

## 2. Claim markers

Put the claim ID in square brackets at the end of the sentence, list item or table cell that relies on it: `[CL-05]`. Where a sentence combines claims, list them: `[CL-01, CL-23]`. Purely editorial sentences, such as transitions and instructions that state no fact, need no marker.

The markers let the editorial, fact and policy reviews check the draft against the dossier. They come out at publishing.

## 3. Don't move a claim

Each of these changes a claim, and needs the dossier to allow it:

- **Strength:** "can help find" to "finds", "typically" to "always", "up to" to a flat figure.
- **Scope:** one suburb to "Perth", two named technicians to "our team", one plan to every plan, one customer's result to "customers".
- **Time:** a figure for a period to the present tense with no period, an old price to "current".
- **Cause:** "after we changed X, Y rose" to "X raised Y".
- **Comparison:** a fact about this business to "faster", "cheaper" or "better than others".
- **Attribution:** an owner's statement presented as independent fact, or an approved quote paraphrased into a different meaning.

Shortening is fine when the permitted meaning and conditions survive. "Annual inspection $295 including GST, for single-storey homes up to 250 m²" can't lose "including GST" or "up to 250 m²".

## 4. Figures, quotes and comparisons

- Keep units, currency, tax basis, period, sample and rounding as the dossier records them. Don't convert, round, total or derive a new figure. A percentage worked out from a count needs the dossier's approval of that percentage.
- Use a quote's exact approved wording, speaker and context. Don't trim it into a different meaning. Paraphrase only where the dossier allows, and attribute the paraphrase.
- Present a comparison only with the comparable versions, conditions and criteria the dossier records, and show the criteria.

## 5. People and first person

Use "we" for the business only for what the business does or did, as recorded. Use "I" only for a named person whose own contribution and permission the dossier records. A source-led page describes its research, for example "the state consumer guide says", and never implies use, a visit or a test.

Name reviewers only for a review they actually did, on a recorded revision, within their expertise. Add no byline, credential, portrait or "reviewed by" line that the dossier doesn't hold.

## 6. Regulated and high-consequence claims

For health, safety, legal, financial and other regulated claims, write only what the dossier clears, with its review status. If the review is pending, the claim stays out or becomes a placeholder naming the reviewer. This skill gives no legal, medical, financial or safety clearance, and doesn't decide whether copy meets any law, advertising code or professional rule.

## 7. Pressure and new information

If the user, the owner or a supplied file asks for a claim the dossier doesn't permit, don't write it. Offer the permitted wording, and send the new claim to the evidence owner as a writer query. An owner's approval sets the business's position but doesn't verify a technical, comparative or regulated claim. A new fact supplied during drafting goes to the dossier owner, and the draft uses a placeholder until the dossier is updated. Record each case in section 6.
